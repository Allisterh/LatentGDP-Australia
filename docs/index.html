About

This page publishes an estimate of Australian quarterly GDP growth with measurement error filtered out of the three ABS measures (expenditure, income and production based GDP). How the estimate is made is described below. Background: RBA Research Discussion Paper 2014-12 (Rees, Lancaster & Finlay).

Data: ABS Cat. 5206.0 Table 24 (Selected Analytical Series), seasonally adjusted, chain volume measures. The page is rebuilt automatically after each quarterly release.

How the estimate is made

Each quarter the ABS publishes three estimates of GDP growth built from different sources: spending (E), incomes (I) and industry production (P). They never agree exactly, because each contains measurement error. The model treats true GDP growth as an unobserved series, treats each published measure as true growth plus its own error, and works out the most likely path of true growth given all three.

It rests on four assumptions:

  • True growth moves around a long-run average and partly persists from one quarter to the next. How volatile it is can change over time. The typical size of a quarterly shock to true growth is estimated at about 0.8 percentage points in the 1980s, 0.3 in the 2010s and 3.4 in 2020 Q2. Because the three measures agreed on the size of the 2020 Q2 fall, the model treats COVID as a genuine collapse in activity rather than a measurement problem.
  • Measurement errors change in size over time. Each measure's errors can be larger in some periods than in others.
  • Errors partly unwind. In the quarter after an error, about 43% of it is reversed for the spending measure, 35% for income and 18% for production.
  • Only some errors are linked. The spending and income measures share some of the same balancing adjustments, so their errors may move together. The production measure is built from separate industry surveys, so its errors are assumed independent of the other two.

The model is estimated on data from 1980 Q1 onward, following the RBA paper, because measurement errors before then may not reflect how the ABS measures behave today. Over that period the estimated true-growth path has 87% of the variance of headline growth, so roughly 13% of the variance of quarterly headline growth is measurement noise. Estimation is Bayesian, so every figure comes with an interval that shows its uncertainty.

Before anything is published, the estimate has to pass automatic checks that the statistical sampling worked properly. If any check fails, this page is not updated.

The model was chosen by comparing a range of alternatives on how well each forecast the next quarter's published figures. The comparison, its caveats and the code are on GitHub.
