About

This page publishes a Bayesian state-space estimate of Australian quarterly GDP growth that filters measurement error out of the three ABS measures (Expenditure, Income, Production-based GDP). The model is m12 from this repo: an AR(1) latent state, measurement errors that follow their own AR(1) with stochastic volatility, and a construction-based zero restriction on the Production-side error covariance. Background: RBA RDP 2014-12 (Rees, Lancaster & Finlay).

Data: ABS Cat. 5206.0 Table 24 (Selected Analytical Series), seasonally adjusted, chain volume measures. The page is rebuilt automatically after each quarterly release.

Model details

Observation equations

ytk = μt + εtk, k = E, I, P εtk = ψk εt-1k + utk (utE, utI) ~ MVN(0, ΣEI,t), utP ~ N(0, σP,t2)

Each measurement error follows an AR(1) with coefficient ψk, so an error in one quarter can be partly carried into, or reversed in, the next. ΣEI,t has standard deviations σE,t, σI,t and a constant correlation ρEI: E and I share national-accounts balancing items. P is built from independent industry surveys, so its error is independent of E and I by construction (the "structural Sigma"). The first quarter's errors start from the stationary distribution at that quarter's variances. With ψ = 0 the model is m9, the previous production model.

Latent state and stochastic volatility

μt = c + ρ · μt-1 + ηt, ηt ~ N(0, τ2), μ1 stationary hk,t = μh,k + φk (hk,t-1 - μh,k) + ωk ξk,t, ξk,t ~ N(0, 1), hk,1 stationary σk,t2 = exp(hk,t) = Var(utk), k = E, I, P

The volatility captures the long fall in measurement error (largest in the 1960s–70s). It does not absorb COVID: the three measures agreed in 2020 Q2, so the collapse goes into μt. The estimated ψ are negative (about −0.5, −0.3 and −0.2 for E, I and P in the research fit on the 2026 Q2 data): the measurement errors partly reverse the next quarter. μ is integrated out by a Kalman filter on the quasi-differenced data yt − ψ yt-1 during sampling, and drawn by forward-filtering backward-sampling.

Priors

In the model's annualised units (400×dlog; this page shows quarter-on-quarter, i.e. divided by 4). N(m, s) has sd s.

c ~ N(1.5, 1) ρ ~ N(0.5, 0.3) constrained to (-1, 1) τ ~ half-N(0, 2) μh,k ~ N(2, 1) φk ~ Uniform(-1, 1) ωk ~ half-N(0, 0.5) ρEI ~ N(0, 0.5) constrained to (-1, 1) ψk ~ N(0, 0.5) constrained to (-1, 1)

All priors for all models: priors.md. Nothing is published unless the fit has <0.1% divergences, E-BFMI ≥ 0.3, R-hat ≤ 1.01 (hyperparameters) / 1.05 (states) and ESS ≥ 400.

Why this specification

The 12 specifications were compared by leave-future-out cross-validation: one-step-ahead predictive density, latent states integrated out, 146 quarters from 1990 Q1 (current-vintage data). They were compared in three groups with different data: the three measures on the full sample (m1–m7, m9, m12); the three measures plus hours from 1978 (m8, m10); and the mixed-frequency m11, which also sees monthly employment inside the predicted quarter.

  • Two features help, and their gains add up: stochastic volatility and serially correlated measurement errors. m12 has both. It beats m9 (volatility only) by 16.8 nats (SE 6.7 assuming independent quarters, 6.1 allowing for serial correlation) and m5 (serial correlation only) by 63 (SE 14 and 21). The gain over m9 is broad: m12 is ahead in 99 of the 146 quarters and by 19.9 nats before 2020. It is behind in 2020, by 4.2 nats.
  • m12 replaced m9 in October 2026 under a rule fixed before its results were known: pass the publication checks on two seeds, have a reliable score, beat m9 by at least 2 serial-correlation-robust SEs, and fit in under 30 minutes. It met all four (z = 2.7).
  • The structural restriction itself is not supported by prediction (m9 against m4, the same model without it: +1.5 nats, SE 1.5). It is kept because it reflects how GDP(P) is built.
  • Neither hours nor monthly employment detectably improves GDP prediction on m12. The re-specified hours model m10 is ahead by 47 nats, but with an SE of 30; the employment model m11 is behind by 8 (SE 27; both SEs allow for serial correlation).

The comparison scores predictions of the observed measures, not the split between true growth and measurement error. Models that predict GDP about equally well can imply very different splits. Over the same 1978–2026 quarters m10's latent path has about 75% of headline variance and m12's about 91% (research-suite fits on the 2026 Q2 data). The variance figure above is therefore a property of m12, not something the comparison confirms.

An earlier PSIS-LOO comparison on this page (m9 +6 over m4, SV +240) was invalid and has been withdrawn.
